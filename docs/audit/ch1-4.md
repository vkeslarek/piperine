## Chapter 1 — Verilog-AMS introduction

Chapter 1 is entirely conceptual (no grammar, no directly testable construct):
conservative systems, Kirchhoff's laws, signal-flow systems, and mixed
conservative/signal-flow systems are all realized indirectly through the constructs
audited in chapters 3-5 (natures/disciplines/branches/contribution statements). No
findings are recorded against chapter 1 itself; its concepts are exercised — and where
divergent, already captured — under the chapter 3/4 rows above (discipline access-name
resolution, branch contribution semantics).

## Chapter 2 — Lexical conventions

The lexer is a clean, deliberately from-scratch implementation of §2.2-2.9, but the
based-number grammar (§2.6.1) has real gaps clustered around three things: **digit-set
validation is not per-base** (over-permissive FORM), **four-state (`x`/`z`/`?`) values
have no representation anywhere in the value/type system** and silently collapse to `0`
at const-eval (the most severe finding in this chapter — a structural absence, not a
corner case), and **whitespace between a based-literal's base letter and its digits is
rejected**, which breaks one of the LRM's own textbook examples. Underscore-placement
rules are slightly over-permissive at two points. Escape sequences (§2.7 Table 2-2),
identifier/escaped-identifier lexing (§2.8/§2.8.1), and item-level attribute-instance
syntax (§2.9.1 Syntax 2-5 through 2-10) are all conformant. Expression-level attribute
instances (§2.9 Examples 6-8, on operators/calls/conditional branches) are entirely
absent from the expression grammar — a real, if narrow, FORM gap.

## Chapter 3 — Data types

Parameter declarations, ranges (`from`/`exclude` with `[`/`(` bound forms and bare
`exclude value`), `localparam`, and `aliasparam` are solidly implemented — the one
alias-typing bug (always `Real`) is minor. The chapter's most consequential gap is
structural, not narrow: **the assignment-pattern token `` '{ `` cannot be lexed at
all**, which blocks parameter-array initializers, string-parameter valid-value lists,
and multidimensional array/string initializers — three separate LRM subsections
(§3.4.4, §3.4.6, §3.4.8) whose own worked examples cannot be parsed. Nature/discipline
declarations, net declarations, and branch declarations parse and elaborate, but the
discipline's `access` attribute — the mechanism chapter 3 defines for naming a
discipline's own accessor functions — is recorded and then never consulted; this
surfaces concretely as the chapter 4 access-function findings below. Genvar, ground,
real-net, and net-compatibility declarations were spot-checked and did not surface
further findings within this pass's scope/time budget (see tally below — several §3.6.x
subsections were not independently re-derived beyond what `annex_a_grammar.rs` and
`docs/spec/part_i_language.md` already document, and are not re-litigated here).

## Chapter 4 — Expressions

The core Pratt-parser expression grammar (arithmetic, relational, logical, bitwise,
reduction, shift, ternary, concatenation/replication) is a faithful, complete
implementation of Table 4-1/4-3's operator set and precedence — no divergence found
there. The real-operand conversion rules (§4.2.1.3, arithmetic promotion to real) are
implemented correctly; only the **real→integer rounding rule (§4.2.1.1)** diverges (it
errors instead of rounding). The built-in math/transcendental function tables (§4.3)
were spot-checked against `crate::math::MATH_FNS` and line up with Table 4-14/4-15 (not
tabulated as a finding — fully conformant). The most severe divergences in this chapter
are in **§4.4 access functions**: only the literal `V`/`I` spellings are recognized —
neither the generic `potential`/`flow` alternative nor any custom discipline's own
`access`-named accessor works, at either the grammar or the elaborator level (the latter
is self-documented in source), and the **port access operator `I(<port>)`** (§4.4/§5.4.3)
has no grammar support whatsoever, blocking the LRM's own diode example. Finally, the
**`laplace_*` operator registry is internally miswired**: two of the four real LRM
variant names (`laplace_zd`, `laplace_nd`) are never registered as callable operators
despite being reserved keywords, while three fabricated, non-LRM names are — directly
contradicting this session's own `docs/spec/part_v_builtins.md`/`part_i_language.md`
claims that all five (sic — the LRM only defines four) variants work. `zi_*`'s
equivalent registration is correct. Signal access functions, analog operators
(`ddt`/`idt`/`idtmod`/`ddx`/`delay`/`transition`/`slew`/`table`/`zi_*`/`ac_stim`), and
user-defined analog functions (§4.7) were otherwise not found to diverge within this
pass's time budget beyond the rows above.
